# Checklist

Every item is a pattern eusouosalmo corrected by hand in a script, with the reason, so the next script arrives without it. Read it at the end, on the finished file. The examples are made up and come from other topics; they show the pattern only. A new correction from the author becomes a new item here, written the same way: the pattern and why, never the facts of the case that taught it.

## Context and idea

- **One video, one idea.** Notes with two concepts become two videos: two ideas in one reel dilute the hook and give the viewer a place to leave.
- **Context known before structure.** Platform, duration and objective change the CTA and the rules: "no próximo vídeo eu falo de..." fits a long video; a spoken "me segue" at the end of a reel cuts the loop.

## Source and facts

- **The knowledge is the subject, not the reading.** A draft that opens with "eu tô lendo..." makes the source the topic. There is always a source; it is not what adds value. It goes to the caption credit.
- **What the source says is checked.** Definitions taken from memory were wrong several times in one script. Every claim about the source that the notes do not back is in `A checar antes de gravar`.
- **Checkable technical facts are checked in the official source**, including the ones the author remembers with confidence (an operator, a flag, a version, a default). The author's memory of the source can be imprecise too.

## The author's story

- **No promotion.** Someone who led a project once is not "o tech lead". Role, number and result stay at the degree of the notes, because a viewer who finds out does not trust the next video.
- **No date, no employer.** "No meu trabalho atual" dates the video and points at a company and its people; it becomes "quando eu precisei", "uma vez".
- **Hedges stay.** A tool the notes give "por exemplo" stays an example, not the only alternative.
- **Ask for the concrete case, never depend on it.** "Usei várias ferramentas" with no names is too vague: ask which ones. When the author has no case, keep the general sentence if it holds or cut the passage. A real case beats a general claim.

## Hook

- **The niche in full words.** The area with its qualifier and the role in full ("desenvolvedor", not "dev"): the viewer decides in the first seconds whether the video is for them.
- **A hook with technique.** A statement about a process ("o deploy só vai num sentido") holds no one: no person, nothing concrete, nothing left open. The hook comes from a pattern in [hooks.md](hooks.md).
- **A true contradiction.** A contradicting hook keeps the qualifier that makes it true, and the video pays it off.

## Body

- **An image explained once.** Reusing the parts of an image ("quem tá no fundo do funil") turns it into vocabulary the listener must translate without the drawing. After the explanation, the audience's own words.
- **Every claim earns its reason.** A claim the viewer would question ("precisa escrever o teste antes", "pode virar um problema") gets a concrete reason or leaves.
- **Connectives where the ear needs them.** Two loose sentences where the second explains the first become one sentence with "porque" or "isso porque". Short loose sentences sound choppy when spoken; a connective the relation does not ask for sounds like filler.
- **No dry or vague line.** A sentence like "Era coisa de quem viu." gets its referent.
- **The author's words, once each.** The author's word for an idea stays, never a synonym. A word that piles up (one noun seven times) or sits twice in a row ("o time virou um time só") gets rewritten.
- **Fits the duration.** At 130 words per minute, 90 s is 195 words; a script over it is cut, not sped up.

## CTA

- **One spoken ask, a question the viewer answers about themselves.** No "me segue" in the speech; it goes in the caption.
- **Answerable without the image.** A CTA that asks the viewer to place themselves on the hook's image ("tu tá no topo ou no fundo do funil?") was corrected twice. The CTA uses the words people use about their own work.
