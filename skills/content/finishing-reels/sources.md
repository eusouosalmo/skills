# Sources

What backs each rule of the skill about punch-ins. **Observed**: measured in the author's own CapCut projects or seen in an eval run. **Preference**: eusouosalmo's call, with where the search for a source went. Research notes are in `docs/research/edicao-de-video-com-ia.md`, section 3.6.

| Rule | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Punch-ins always, with the reference project's scale (about 1.6x), as a hard cut held 0.6 to 1.0 s on a key word | The author's Jev-AI project: clips of the main track at 1.59x (6.73 s, "Jev") and 1.68x (12.2 s, "OpenAI"), 0.7 and 1.0 s, no keyframe; eusouosalmo chose "sempre" (2026-10-17). No platform or study gives a scale, length or count: tools use 1.15 to 1.3x, without data; the one dataset on short videos (Xue et al. 2026, preprint, correlational) finds moderate stimulation engages more than heavy, and measures shot changes only | Observed (the author's style); scale and length have no primary source |
| Only where the face fills the frame, never in a split screen | eusouosalmo (2026-10-17): "só faz sentido no rosto inteiro [...] senão divide a atenção". No study tests a zoom on the face beside a diagram. Indirect evidence points the same way: abrupt onsets and looming objects capture attention (Yantis and Jonides 1984; Franconeri and Simons 2003); the instructor's face takes 22 to 26% of gaze at 7% of the screen, and the gain from showing it vanished on hard content, attributed to split attention (Wang and Antonenko 2017); toggling the face raised cognitive load (Kizilcec, Bailenson and Gomez 2015). In the Jev project every visible punch-in is on the full-frame face; the one at 95.8 s sits under a full-screen scene | Supported in part (inference from lab and lecture studies, not reels) |
| Never on a shot that is already a close-up | Whatsapp reel: the closing shot already shows the face from forehead to chin; the baseline zoomed it to 125% | Observed |
| Take the timeline's suggested punch-in | Whatsapp reel: `timeline.md` asks 110% on "Mark Zuckerberg" so the finger stays under the photo it points at | Observed |
| The punch-in's own sound on its first frame, at the reference volume | Jev-AI: a mouse click on the frame of the first punch-in. A short sound in sync with a visual change makes that change pop out (Van der Burg et al. 2008, "pip and pop"); TikTok's creative guidance asks for effects in sync with sound, without numbers | Supported (lab study, applied by inference); the kind of sound is the author's style |
| No library sound over the panel's sfx | The panel's `00-sfx.wav` already gives each event the sound of its material (the author's style notes); a sound matched one to one with an event did not hurt learning, while repeated arbitrary sounds did (Moreno and Mayer 2000) | Supported in part |
| Leave the dark wedge of frame 0 | The posted reel exported by CapCut shows the same wedge in the top-right corner of frame 0; two baseline runs "fixed" it and drifted from CapCut | Observed |
| Lower the scale only as far as it takes for the head, hair included, to clear the overlays | eusouosalmo (2026-10-17), shown the celular hook at 1.2x and at 1.63x with the hair under the comment print: "vale livre". Searched `docs/research/edicao-de-video-com-ia.md` 3.6 and `texto-na-tela.md`: no source on a zoom running into an overlay | Preference |
