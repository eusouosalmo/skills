# Searched images and clips

Read in step 2 when the plan will show a person, brand, product, page or third-party clip the speech names.

## What is searched, and when

In the breakdown, before the plan's gate, so the author approves the real picture with its source:

- a photo of a public person the speech names;
- a brand's or product's logo, a product, a public page, a cover;
- a stretch of third-party footage (film, game, keynote): only its URL and span, never the file.

Proof only the author has (their test, their screen) and the author's own recording stay asked for, never searched.

## Where to look

| What | Where, in order | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Logo, product | The brand's own brand or press page; only versions the brand publishes, its monochrome one included | Recoloured to the design system, distorted, redrawn |
| Public person | Wikimedia Commons, Flickr with a CC licence, then the official press kit | Agencies (Getty, Reuters), Google Images, third-party profiles |
| Public page | A source shot of the page itself (see [formats.md](formats.md)) | A copy hosted elsewhere |
| Footage | The official channel (the company's, the official trailer, the keynote) | A re-upload |

## What goes in the asset list and at the gate

Save the file in `assets/`, named after what it shows (`assets/mark-zuckerberg.jpg`). In the asset list, for each one: the URL of the page it came from, the author, the licence and, when the licence asks for it, the credit line. Copy that credit line into "Créditos para a legenda" in `timeline.md`: credit goes in the post caption, not on the scene (Creative Commons accepts credit "in any reasonable manner" for the medium). Show the image at the gate with these data, to confirm or swap.

For footage: the official URL and the exact span (start and end, a few seconds), located inside the video by its transcript or chapters; a URL without the span leaves the author searching a two-hour keynote. The author records or downloads it; download it yourself only when the source offers a download (press kit, Commons). Say that film, game and music clips often trip Instagram's rights detection.

When the speech refers to someone else's public demonstration ("the company showed it running"), the official video of it may serve as proof: offer it at the gate as an option, beside asking the author.

## Limits

- **Person:** the photo only identifies whom the speech names. Cutting out and removing the background are fine; changing the face or expression, a meme, or placing them next to a product or offer are not. A free licence covers the photographer, not the person's image.
- **Reel that sells:** when the reel is sponsored or ends on a CTA for the author's own product (course, mentoring), ask the author for any person's image instead of searching. Informative use of a public figure is accepted; commercial use is not. The limit covers the person's image only: a clip the speech quotes (the keynote moment it retells) is still found, with its URL and span, and offered at the gate.
- **Logo:** cited without commercial connotation, as the brand publishes it. A Commons licence does not cover the trademark.

## Nothing usable

Ask at the gate, multiple choice: ask the author, with a labelled visible placeholder (recommended); or an illustration in the design system's style (for a person, a generic icon, never a face).
