# Checklist

Every item is a pattern eusouosalmo corrected by hand in a caption, with the reason, so the next caption arrives without it. Read it at the end, on the finished caption. The examples are made up and come from other topics; they show the pattern only. A new correction from the author becomes a new item here, written the same way: the pattern and why, never the facts of the case that taught it.

## Checked by scripts/check-caption.py

- No URL where caption links are not clickable.
- Hashtags within the platform's maximum.
- No ask for a specific word, a tag, a share or a like.
- No note on how a fact was checked ("seção 4.2", "página 37", "conferido na documentação") outside the source line.
- No caption sentence that repeats a spoken line almost word for word, the hook and the spoken ask included.

## Checked by you

- **The first line names the topic and leaves a question open.** A first line that copies the hook ("Teste que passa de primeira não testa nada.") tells the viewer what they just heard; one that gives the answer closes the reason to tap "mais". "Testes automatizados: por que o primeiro teste precisa falhar?" names the topic and leaves the question.
- **The caption adds, it does not retell.** Each paragraph carries something the speech did not: the exact command the video could only describe ("git rebase --onto", where the speech said "um rebase diferente"), the caveat, the next step.
- **One ask, and not the video's.** When the speech closes with a question for the comments, the caption asks for something else, usually to follow. Two calls asking for the same thing compete with each other.
- **The follow ask has the handle and a reason.** "Me segue no @eusouosalmo pra ver o próximo" turns into a link to the profile and says what the reader gets. A bare "me segue" gives neither.
- **The source is a credit, not a second ask.** "Fonte: <obra>, de <autor> (link na bio)" on its own line. "Corre lá no link da bio pra comprar o livro" next to a follow ask makes two asks.
- **The fact, not the proof.** "O cache expira em 5 minutos, tá na doc, seção 4.2" carries the author's check into the reader's text. The reader gets the fact; where it was checked goes to "Para o autor". The source line is the one place for whoever wants to check.
- **Facts come only from the script and the notes.** A detail you know and the script lacks (a fourth option of a command, a definition from the source) stays out of the caption, even flagged: once posted, the flag is gone. Offer it in "Para o autor" as a suggestion; the author adds it after checking.
